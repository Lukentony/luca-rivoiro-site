Backend / Web application

HomeSync

Household tasks with due dates, turns and points: an app two people use every day, with a FastAPI backend and the data on a home server.

In daily use, by two of us

The problem

Shared between two, housework goes missing in three places: whose turn it is, whether it is overdue, and what was actually done. A sheet on the fridge cannot hold the history, and the apps that do want a subscription and your data on a server that is not yours.

My contribution

I wrote the FastAPI backend and the interface, and kept out of the project everything that does not serve two people living together. The parts that took the most work are the event ledger, where every completion and every correction are events and the balances are derived from them, never recalculated backwards, and working without a network. Concretely:

  • FastAPI backend with SQLite in WAL mode, no separate database service;
  • turn-based assignment, with late penalties applied once per due date;
  • per-event history, with stated corrections that do not rewrite the past;
  • offline queue: ticks made without a network stay queued and leave on their own, with no duplicates;
  • interface served already compiled: 4 requests and 0.6 MB, against 24 and 3.6 MB before.

Available evidence

15 backend test files, 21 browser tests that open the app for real at two screen sizes, and a test that cuts the network and checks the tick stays queued and leaves when the connection returns. The home database holds 433 recorded completions across 75 tasks and 8 rooms.

Usage and limits

Not a product: it is a home app. SQLite holds up with two people and one server, not a block of flats. The language model is off by default: Italian questions go through deterministic rules and the model remains the last resort. The public repository is a copy cleaned of data, frozen at an earlier version: it shows the project, not the running code. The app writes to no external service: it reads addresses and opens pages.
